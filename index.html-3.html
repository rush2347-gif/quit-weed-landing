The Science Behind Quitting

🧠 Effects on Brain Development
Harvard Medical School, 2022
Regular cannabis use during adolescence and young adulthood can impair brain development, particularly in areas responsible for decision-making, memory, and attention. Users report measurable cognitive improvements within 2-4 weeks of quitting.
⚡ Dopamine & Reward System
Nature Neuroscience, 2021
THC artificially elevates dopamine levels. Over time, your brain adapts by reducing natural dopamine production, making everyday activities feel less rewarding. Quitting allows your dopamine system to recalibrate within 30-60 days.
🔄 Bipolar Disorder & Cannabis
Journal of Affective Disorders, 2023
People with bipolar disorder who use cannabis experience 2-3x more frequent mood episodes, longer depressive periods, and reduced medication effectiveness. Quitting significantly improves mood stability and treatment outcomes.
💭 Anxiety & Paranoia
American Journal of Psychiatry, 2022
Regular cannabis use is associated with increased anxiety, panic attacks, and paranoia. These symptoms typically resolve within 1-2 weeks of quitting as the brain's anxiety system rebalances.

⚠️ Special Note for People with Bipolar Disorder

If you have bipolar disorder, cannabis use is particularly risky. Research shows it:

• Triggers manic episodes — THC's dopamine spike can activate mania
• Deepens depression — Withdrawal crashes are more severe
• Blocks medication — Cannabis interferes with mood stabilizers
• Increases suicide risk — Studies show higher rates in people with bipolar + cannabis use

If you have bipolar disorder and want to quit, working with a psychiatrist is especially important. This tool can help with daily intention, but medical support is essential.
